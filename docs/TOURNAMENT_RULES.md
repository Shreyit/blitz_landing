# SPORTS TOURNAMENT — RULES & REGULATIONS

**Six-Team Tournament | Two-Pool Format**

> **Draft for organizing-committee approval.** Rules marked as recommended or proposed are event-specific interpretations and should be confirmed before distributing the final regulations.

## 1. General Tournament Structure

The tournament will consist of **six participating teams**, divided into two pools:

| Pool A | Pool B |
|---|---|
| A1 | B1 |
| A2 | B2 |
| A3 | B3 |

The same pool structure will be followed for **Football, Cricket, Tug of War, Dodgeball, Badminton, Chess and Table Tennis**.

### Pool-stage fixtures

**Pool A**
1. A1 vs A2
2. A2 vs A3
3. A3 vs A1

**Pool B**
1. B1 vs B2
2. B2 vs B3
3. B3 vs B1

Each team plays **two pool matches**. The first-ranked team in each pool qualifies directly for the Final:

**Final: Pool A Winner vs Pool B Winner**

There will be **no Quarterfinals, Semifinals, third-place matches or byes**.

Men's and Women's Tug of War are separate competitions, each with its own pool standings and Final. Powerlifting is a separate weight-category competition, not a pool-versus-pool event.

## 2. Common Pool Ranking System

| Match result | Ranking points |
|---|---:|
| Win | 3 |
| Draw, where permitted | 1 |
| Loss | 0 |

These are **tournament-ranking points**, separate from goals, runs, games, sets, pulls, eliminations or other in-match statistics.

**Common tie-breaking principle:**
1. Rank teams by total pool-ranking points.
2. If exactly two teams are tied, use their head-to-head result first.
3. If that does not resolve the tie, use the sport-specific statistic listed in its section.
4. If all three teams are tied, apply sport-specific statistics directly rather than attempting a circular head-to-head ranking.
5. If a statistic narrows the tie to two teams, their head-to-head result may be reconsidered; if still unresolved, follow the published sporting tie-break procedure.

All tie-breaking methods must be announced **before** pool matches begin.

## 3. Football

### Team composition

- Each team fields **five players at a time**.
- At least **one female player must be on the field throughout the match**.
- Rolling substitutions are allowed from registered substitutes.
- A female player being substituted off must be replaced by another female player to maintain the requirement.
- The organizing committee must announce each team's permitted squad size before registration closes.

### Match duration

- First half: **10 minutes**
- Halftime: **3 minutes**
- Second half: **10 minutes**
- Total playing time: **20 minutes**

The referee controls timekeeping, stoppages and substitutions.

### Pool-stage scoring

- Win: 3 ranking points
- Draw: 1 ranking point for each team
- Loss: 0 ranking points

Draws are permitted during pool matches.

### Ranking the pool

Use, in order:
1. Pool-ranking points
2. Head-to-head result (two-team tie)
3. Goal Difference (GD)
4. Goals Scored (GF)
5. A pre-announced deciding procedure if still tied

**Formula:**

`Goal Difference = Goals Scored − Goals Conceded`

**Example:**

| Team | Ranking points | Goals scored | Goals conceded | Goal Difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A1 | 3 | 4 | 2 | +2 |
| A2 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 0 |
| A3 | 3 | 2 | 4 | -2 |

All three teams have equal ranking points, so **A1** finishes first with GD **+2**.

### Final

- Pool A Winner vs Pool B Winner
- Two 10-minute halves and a 3-minute halftime
- If the Final ends level, conduct a penalty shootout.
- Each team takes **three initial penalties**, followed by sudden-death penalties if necessary.

If an unresolved pool tie involves three teams, the organizing committee must define the precise deciding mini-playoff or ranking procedure beforehand; a two-team penalty shootout alone cannot resolve a three-way tie fairly.

## 4. Cricket

### Team composition

- **Six players** per team.
- At least **one female player must be included in the playing six and remain on the field**, subject to an approved injury replacement.
- All participants must be registered.

### Venue and match format

- Venue: **OC Basketball Court**.
- Each match consists of **two innings**.
- Each innings has a maximum of **six overs**.
- Each over comprises **six legal deliveries**.
- An innings ends after six overs or when the batting side cannot continue because **five wickets** have fallen.

### Bowling rules

- **Overarm bowling** is mandatory.
- A bowler may bowl **no more than two overs per innings**.
- At least **one complete over must be bowled by a female player**.
- Therefore, at least three bowlers are needed for a six-over innings.
- A **no-ball** awards one extra run and a **free hit** on the next legal delivery.
- **Proposed local wide rule:** a wide awards one extra run and must be rebowled.
- Wides and no-balls are not legal deliveries.

### Court scoring zones

| Event | Runs |
|---|---:|
| Ball reaches the designated 1-run area | 1 |
| Ball reaches the designated 2-run area | 2 |
| Ball bounces before hitting/crossing the front net | 4 |
| Ball directly hits the front net or travels beyond the designated front boundary without bouncing | 6 |

The 1-, 2-, 4- and 6-run zones must be clearly marked and explained before play begins. For a scoring stroke, apply the relevant boundary or zone award, rather than adding overlapping awards. Local rules must also define the treatment of side nets, catches after net contact, run-outs and other unusual court situations.

### Pool-stage scoring

- Win: 3 ranking points
- Loss: 0 ranking points
- A tied match proceeds to a **Super Over**.

### Super Over

- Each team bats for one additional over.
- Two wickets end a team's Super Over innings.
- The team scoring more runs wins.
- If tied again, play further Super Overs until there is a winner, subject to the announced event procedures.

### Ranking the pool

1. Pool-ranking points
2. Head-to-head result (two-team tie)
3. **Net Run Rate (NRR)**
4. Deciding contest if still unresolved

**Formula:**

`NRR = (Total Runs Scored ÷ Total Overs Faced) − (Total Runs Conceded ÷ Total Overs Bowled)`

**Example:** A team scores 72 runs in 6 overs and concedes 60 runs in 6 overs.

`NRR = (72 ÷ 6) − (60 ÷ 6) = 12 − 10 = +2.000`

For the overall pool NRR, sum runs and overs across **both matches** before dividing. Do not average the two match-level NRR values.

Important:
- If a team is all out before its allotted six overs, use the **full six overs** for that batting innings when calculating NRR.
- For a successful chase, use the **actual legal deliveries faced**.
- Convert cricket over notation correctly: **4.3 overs = 4 overs and 3 balls = 4.5 decimal overs**.
- Exclude Super Over runs and deliveries from NRR.
- Any abandoned/no-result and reduced-over policies should be announced in advance.

### Final

Pool A Winner vs Pool B Winner, played under the same six-over court-cricket rules. A tie is resolved through the announced Super Over procedure.

## 5. Tug of War

### Team composition

| Category | Players per team |
|---|---:|
| Men's Tug of War | 8 men |
| Women's Tug of War | 5 women |

Men and women compete in **separate pool competitions** and have separate Finals.

### Match format

- Each encounter is **best of three valid pulls**.
- The first team to win **two pulls** wins the encounter.
- Each pull lasts a maximum of **30 seconds**.
- If neither team wins within 30 seconds, declare that pull a **draw and repeat it**.
- A referee validates successful pulls.
- Adequate rest should be provided between repeated pulls.

### Rules and fouls

- The rope must not be tied, knotted or locked around a participant's body.
- Pulling must use the prescribed underarm technique.
- Elbows may not drop below knees.
- Dangerous falls, illegal grips or other unsafe conduct can result in a foul.
- The referee may order a replay, warn participants or award a pull according to the previously published foul policy.
- Rope, pulling surface and footwear must be inspected for safety.

### Pool-stage scoring

- Encounter win: 3 ranking points
- Encounter loss: 0 ranking points

### Ranking the pool

1. Pool-ranking points
2. Head-to-head result (two-team tie)
3. **Pull Difference (PD)**
4. Total valid pulls won
5. Deciding encounter if necessary

**Formula:**

`Pull Difference = Pulls Won − Pulls Lost`

**Example:**

| Team | Ranking points | Pulls won | Pulls lost | Pull Difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A1 | 3 | 3 | 2 | +1 |
| A2 | 3 | 2 | 3 | -1 |
| A3 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 0 |

**A1** finishes first with the best Pull Difference (+1). Drawn pulls that are replayed are not counted as wins or losses.

### Finals

- Men's Final: Pool A Men's Winner vs Pool B Men's Winner
- Women's Final: Pool A Women's Winner vs Pool B Women's Winner
- Both follow the same best-of-three valid-pulls format.

*Note: This timed best-of-three format is a custom event rule; it should not be represented as the official international round-robin format.*

## 6. Dodgeball

### Team composition

- Each team registers **eight players**.
- At least **three female players must be in the registered squad**.
- **Four players from each team** are on court at any time.
- Only **one dodgeball** is used.
- **Recommended clarification for approval:** at least one female player among the four active players. The supplied rule requires three women in the squad, but does not explicitly require a woman on court at all times.

### Match duration and victory

- Each match has a **seven-minute attack/play period**.
- A team wins immediately if it legally eliminates all four opposing active players.
- If time expires with players remaining on both sides, the team with **more surviving active players** wins.
- If both sides have an equal number of surviving players at full time, use a **sudden-death extension**, with the first valid elimination determining the winner.

The surviving-player and sudden-death provisions are proposed clarifications for this event.

### Proposed elimination and safety rules

- A player struck by a legally thrown ball before it touches the ground is eliminated.
- A clean catch before the ball touches the ground eliminates the thrower.
- Deliberate headshots are prohibited.
- Stepping outside the court boundary may result in elimination.
- Physical obstruction/contact with an opponent is prohibited.
- Eliminated players cannot re-enter the same match, except to correct a referee error.
- Substitutes may replace players between matches or in approved injury circumstances, if allowed by the committee.
- Use a suitable, safe dodgeball.

### Pool-stage scoring

- Win: 3 ranking points
- Loss: 0 ranking points

### Ranking the pool

1. Pool-ranking points
2. Head-to-head result (two-team tie)
3. **Elimination Difference (ED)**
4. Total opponents eliminated
5. Deciding match if still tied

**Formula:**

`Elimination Difference = Opponents Eliminated − Own Players Eliminated`

**Example:** If A1 eliminates all four A2 players but loses one of its own players:

`ED = 4 − 1 = +3`

Add Elimination Difference across **both pool matches**.

### Final

Pool A Winner vs Pool B Winner. The same seven-minute period and proposed sudden-death provisions apply.

## 7. Badminton

### Categories — Doubles Only

There are **no singles** events.

| Rubber | Category | Players per team |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Men's Doubles (MD) | 2 men |
| 2 | Women's Doubles (WD) | 2 women |
| 3 | Mixed Doubles (XD) | 1 man + 1 woman |

A team needs at least **two registered men and two registered women**, provided the same eligible players may also play Mixed Doubles.

### Team encounter format

Each team encounter comprises **three doubles rubbers**. The team winning **at least two** rubbers wins the overall encounter.

**All three rubbers must be completed in pool encounters**, even after one side wins the first two, so every team has comparable tie-break statistics.

### Event-specific scoring format

**Pool-stage rubbers:**
- Best of 3 games per doubles rubber
- Each game played to **15 points**
- Win by at least 2 points

**Final rubbers:**
- Best of 3 games per doubles rubber
- Each game played to **21 points**
- Win by at least 2 points

No maximum-point cap is specified in this draft; the committee should decide whether a cap is needed for scheduling.

The 15-point pool-stage rule is **custom to this event** and must be clearly communicated.

### Pool-stage example

| Category | Winner |
|---|---|
| Men's Doubles | A1 |
| Women's Doubles | A2 |
| Mixed Doubles | A1 |

A1 wins the encounter **2–1** in rubbers and receives **3 ranking points**.

### Ranking the pool

1. Pool-ranking points
2. Head-to-head result (two-team tie)
3. **Rubber Difference (RD)**
4. **Game Difference (GD)**
5. **Point Difference (PD)**
6. Pre-announced deciding contest

**Rubber Difference:** `RD = Rubbers Won − Rubbers Lost`

| Team | Ranking points | Rubbers won | Rubbers lost | RD |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A1 | 3 | 4 | 2 | +2 |
| A2 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 0 |
| A3 | 3 | 2 | 4 | -2 |

A1 qualifies with the highest RD (+2).

**Game Difference:** `GD = Total Games Won − Total Games Lost`

For example, eight games won and six lost gives GD **+2**.

**Point Difference:** `PD = Total Points Scored − Total Points Conceded`

Record all individual game scores, including those in lost rubbers, for accurate calculations.

### Substitutions

Substitutions are permitted only:
- Before a rubber begins, with committee approval; or
- For an injury, with committee approval.

Free substitutions during a rubber are not permitted.

### Final

Pool A Winner vs Pool B Winner. The Final includes Men's Doubles, Women's Doubles and Mixed Doubles, each best of three games to **21 points**. The team winning **at least two rubbers** is the Badminton Champion.

## 8. Chess

### Participation and revised format

- **One registered chess player represents each team**.
- Chess follows the two pools of three players rather than the original Swiss format.
- Each player plays the other **two players in their pool**.
- The earlier four Swiss rounds, top-four progression, Semifinals, Buchholz and Cumulative tie-breaks **do not apply** under this revised format.

### Pool fixtures

Pool A: A1 vs A2; A2 vs A3; A3 vs A1. Pool B follows the same pattern.

### Time control and ranking points

- Normal pool game time control: **10 minutes per player**, with no increment.
- Win: **3 ranking points**.
- Draw: **1 ranking point**.
- Loss: **0 ranking points**.

This is the tournament's custom 3–1–0 standings system. For deciding playoff games and the Final series, ordinary chess game points (win = 1, draw = 0.5, loss = 0) are used.

### Ranking the pool

1. Total pool-ranking points
2. Head-to-head result (two-player tie)
3. Chess playoff if still unresolved

**Example:**

| Player | Wins | Draws | Losses | Pool-ranking points |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A1 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 4 |
| A2 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 4 |
| A3 | 0 | 0 | 2 | 0 |

If A1 and A2 drew with one another, head-to-head does not resolve the tie, so they play a tie-break.

### Two-player tie-break

- Play **two blitz games**.
- Time control: **3 minutes + 2 seconds increment per move**.
- Players alternate colours.
- A win is worth 1 chess game point, a draw 0.5 and a loss 0.
- Higher combined game score qualifies.
- If still tied, play an **Armageddon** game with colour allocation, clock times and draw odds announced before the playoffs. Under the proposed Armageddon rule, Black advances in the event of a draw.

### Three-player tie-break

- Conduct a **three-player blitz round-robin**.
- Each player plays both opponents.
- Highest scoring player qualifies.
- If the top rank remains tied, continue with a pre-announced additional playoff procedure until one player qualifies.

### Final

The Pool A winner plays the Pool B winner in a **three-game series**:
- **10 minutes per player** for each normal Final game.
- Win = 1 game point, draw = 0.5, loss = 0.
- Highest combined score after three games becomes Champion.
- If tied, use two games at **3 minutes + 2 seconds increment**, then a pre-announced Armageddon game if necessary.

*Note: Under FIDE time-control terminology, a game of 10 minutes per player without an increment is classified as blitz.*

## 9. Table Tennis

### Categories — Doubles Only

There are **no singles** events.

| Rubber | Category | Players per team |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Men's Doubles (MD) | 2 men |
| 2 | Women's Doubles (WD) | 2 women |
| 3 | Mixed Doubles (XD) | 1 man + 1 woman |

A team requires at least two men and two women, assuming registered players may also participate in Mixed Doubles.

### Team encounter format

Every encounter consists of three doubles rubbers. The side winning **two or three** rubbers wins the encounter and earns **3 pool-ranking points**. All three rubbers must be completed in pool encounters for tie-breaking purposes.

### Event-specific scoring format

**Pool Stage:**
- Best of **3 games** per doubles rubber
- Each game to **15 points**
- Win by **2 points**

**Final:**
- Best of **3 games** per doubles rubber
- Each game to **21 points**
- Win by **2 points**

These are **custom event scoring rules** and differ from standard table tennis games to 11 points. Standard doubles serving order, alternating returns and fault rules apply unless locally modified. The committee should confirm whether there will be a maximum-point cap.

### Example encounter

| Category | A1 games won | A2 games won | Rubber winner |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Men's Doubles | 2 | 0 | A1 |
| Women's Doubles | 1 | 2 | A2 |
| Mixed Doubles | 2 | 1 | A1 |

A1 wins the overall encounter **2–1 in rubbers** and earns **3 pool-ranking points**.

### Ranking the pool

1. Pool-ranking points
2. Head-to-head result (two-team tie)
3. Rubber Difference (RD)
4. Game Difference (GD)
5. Point Difference (PD)
6. Pre-announced deciding contest

**Formulas:**

- `RD = Rubbers Won − Rubbers Lost`
- `GD = Games Won − Games Lost`
- `PD = Total Points Scored − Total Points Conceded`

**Example:**

| Team | Ranking points | Rubbers won | Rubbers lost | RD |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A1 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 0 |
| A2 | 3 | 4 | 2 | +2 |
| A3 | 3 | 2 | 4 | -2 |

A2 qualifies with the highest RD (+2). If RD is tied, use GD and then PD. Every game's actual points must be recorded.

### Substitutions

- Only before a rubber starts, with committee approval, or for an approved injury.
- No unrestricted substitutions during an ongoing rubber.

### Final

Pool A Winner vs Pool B Winner. The Final consists of Men's Doubles, Women's Doubles and Mixed Doubles. Rubbers are best of three games to **21 points**, and the team winning at least two rubbers becomes Champion.

## 10. Powerlifting — Deadlift

### Format

Powerlifting is a **separate individual strength competition**, not a two-pool knockout event.

- Event: **Deadlift only**.
- Separate **men's and women's divisions**.
- Competitors are allocated to weight categories by weigh-in on event day.
- Every lifter gets **three attempts**.
- The highest **successful** deadlift determines the result.

### Weight categories

For a non-overlapping category system, the draft interprets the original categories as:

| Category | Body weight |
|---|---|
| Under 53 kg | Below 53 kg |
| Under 59 kg | 53 kg to below 59 kg |
| Under 66 kg | 59 kg to below 66 kg |
| Under 74 kg | 66 kg to below 74 kg |
| Under 79 kg | 74 kg to below 79 kg |
| Open / 79 kg+ | 79 kg and above |

Use these categories independently for men's and women's events. **The interpretation of Open as 79 kg+ requires committee confirmation.**

### Team participation

- Each team may register **at most one male and one female lifter in each weight category**.
- A lifter may compete only within their designated gender division and approved body-weight category.
- Official event-day weigh-in determines eligibility.

### Three-attempt and locked-weight rules

**Attempt 1**
- The participant declares and locks the first attempt weight.
- The referee judges whether the lift succeeds.

**Attempt 2**
- The participant may request the same or a heavier weight.
- **The declared weight may not be lower than the first attempt**, even if the first attempt failed.

**Attempt 3**
- The participant may request the same or a heavier weight than Attempt 2.
- The declared weight may not decrease from Attempt 2, even if Attempt 2 failed.

**Example:**

| Attempt | Weight | Result |
|---|---:|---|
| 1 | 80 kg | Successful |
| 2 | 90 kg | Unsuccessful |
| 3 | 90 kg | Successful |

The final recorded result is **90 kg**. A participant with no successful attempts has **no valid result**.

The non-decreasing declared-weight rule is a **local event requirement**, not a statement of all federation rules.

### Valid deadlift

- The bar starts on the floor.
- Lift under control until fully upright.
- Knees and hips must be locked out at completion.
- Wait for the referee's down command.
- Lower the bar under control.
- The referee determines validity and applies the announced fault criteria.

### Winner and tie-breaks

1. **Highest successful deadlift weight** wins each category.
2. If tied, the **lighter lifter at official weigh-in** ranks higher.
3. If body weights are exactly equal, the competitor who **achieved the tied weight first** ranks higher.

### Safety

- A qualified referee and trained platform/loading staff are required.
- Inspect all equipment and the lifting area.
- Allow adequate warm-up.
- Do not pressure competitors into unsafe attempts.
- Ensure medical assistance is available.

### Awards

Six body-weight categories for men and six for women create **12 potential category champions**. There is no Pool A vs Pool B Powerlifting Final.

## 11. Consolidated Ranking Criteria

| Sport | Pool ranking priorities |
|---|---|
| Football | Pool Points → Head-to-Head (two-way) → Goal Difference → Goals Scored |
| Cricket | Pool Points → Head-to-Head (two-way) → Net Run Rate |
| Tug of War | Pool Points → Head-to-Head (two-way) → Pull Difference → Pulls Won |
| Dodgeball | Pool Points → Head-to-Head (two-way) → Elimination Difference → Total Eliminations |
| Badminton | Pool Points → Head-to-Head (two-way) → Rubber Difference → Game Difference → Point Difference |
| Chess | Pool Points → Head-to-Head (two-way) → Chess Tie-Break Playoff |
| Table Tennis | Pool Points → Head-to-Head (two-way) → Rubber Difference → Game Difference → Point Difference |
| Powerlifting | Highest Valid Deadlift → Lower Body Weight → Earlier Successful Attempt |

If all three teams are tied on pool points, apply the sport-specific statistical tie-breaker directly. Any residual tie must be resolved by the pre-announced sport-specific deciding procedure.

## 13. General Organizing Rules

1. **Registration:** All players must be registered before their events. Unregistered participants may not compete.
2. **Gender participation:** Teams must comply with the stated sport-specific requirements.
3. **Reporting:** Teams must arrive by the announced reporting deadline. A walkover grace period must be published in advance.
4. **Substitutions:** Only as permitted under the relevant sport's rules.
5. **Walkovers:** A failure to report or field the required players may result in a walkover. Publish default score and ranking-stat treatment before play begins.
6. **Referee authority:** Referees decide matters within matches; administrative or disciplinary appeals follow the committee's announced procedure.
7. **Misconduct:** Unsafe play, abuse, deliberate violations or interference with officials may result in warnings, penalties or disqualification.
8. **Safety:** Appropriate equipment, safe venues and sport-specific precautions are mandatory.
9. **Results:** Record match winners **and** all statistics required for tie-breaking (runs/overs, goals, rubbers, games, points, pulls, eliminations and so on).
10. **Qualification:** Only the first-ranked team from each pool reaches the relevant Final.
11. **Champions:** Each pool-based sport has a Final winner; Powerlifting champions are determined by division and weight category.
12. **No bronze match:** No third-place playoff will be held in pool-based sports.

## 14. Tournament Format Summary

### Pool-based sports

**6 Teams → 2 Pools of 3 → Round-Robin Pool Matches → Top Team from Each Pool → Final → Champion**

### Powerlifting

**Registration → Weigh-In → Weight Category → Three Deadlift Attempts → Highest Valid Lift → Category Champion**
